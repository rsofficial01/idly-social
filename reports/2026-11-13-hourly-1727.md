Caption (passed Gate 2 with the reviewer's fixes applied):

```
A finance company can repossess a vehicle over a loan default only through a civil court or an arbitrator, not by force, the Allahabad High Court has held. A bench of Justices J.J. Munir and Indrajeet Shukla ruled on 26 October; Bar & Bench, LawChakra and NewsX reported the judgment on Friday 13 November.

The case was brought by a transporter whose goods carrier was financed by HDB Financial Services, repayable in 45 monthly instalments. He says he had paid 31 when men acting for the lender stopped him near Chandauli on 9 August 2026 and took the vehicle; his petition alleges they did it at gunpoint. HDB told the court the seizure was over his brother's defaulted loan, which he had guaranteed, and that his own cheques had bounced. The court noted that the company had gone to an arbitrator against the brother but had started no such case against him.

Repossession "has to be done through a process known to law" and "cannot be done by force employing goons, styled as agents", the court said. It ordered the vehicle returned without any pre-condition, the loan restructured at the usual interest with no penal interest, and ₹10,000 in costs paid by the lender. If he defaults again, the company can act only through a civil court or the arbitrator.

The bench relied on the Supreme Court's 16 September ruling in Hari Dutta Sharma v State of UP, which awarded a truck owner ₹10 lakh after his lender took the truck at about 1 am without notice, and asked the RBI to make banks and NBFCs follow its recovery rules. A High Court ruling binds the courts in its own state; a Supreme Court ruling binds every court in India.

Has a recovery agent ever called or come home to you or your family over an EMI?
```
