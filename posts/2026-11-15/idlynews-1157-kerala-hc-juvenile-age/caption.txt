The Kerala High Court has said it is "high time for the legislatures to consider appropriate amendments" to the laws on juveniles and children in conflict with law, to bring the age threshold down from 18 to 16. Justice A. Badharudeen made the observation on Friday, 13 November, citing "the present trend showing sufficient maturity by children" at 16, Bar & Bench and LiveLaw report. The remark is addressed to legislatures and binds no one.

It came in a journalist's petition alleging that bars in Kerala serve liquor to people under 23, the state's legal drinking age under the Kerala Abkari Act. The petitioner sought electronic ID checks, action against bars' licences and a vigilance probe of excise officials. The court directed the Excise Commissioner to file a statement on the steps taken to enforce the ban and listed the case for 24 November.

The Juvenile Justice Act, 2015 is a central law passed by Parliament. Under its Section 15, a Juvenile Justice Board already assesses 16- to 18-year-olds accused of heinous offences, those carrying a minimum of seven years in jail, and they may then be tried as adults.
