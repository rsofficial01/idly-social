NASA's Voyager 1 reaches one light-day from Earth on Wednesday, 18 November, at 3:46 PM IST (10:16 UTC, per NASA's countdown). That's about 25.9 billion km: a radio signal now needs a full 24 hours to cross the gap each way, so a command from NASA and Voyager's reply take about two days.

Voyager 1 launched on 5 September 1977 and is the most distant human-made object. More than 49 years on, it still sends science data home, though NASA has been switching instruments off to stretch its fading power.

To mark the moment, NASA is asking people everywhere to flash "VGR", Voyager's call sign, at the sky in Morse code after sunset on Tuesday, 17 November: V is dot-dot-dot-dash, G is dash-dash-dot, R is dot-dash-dot, repeated for about a minute. A torch works, or NASA's phone flasher at go.nasa.gov/vgr.
