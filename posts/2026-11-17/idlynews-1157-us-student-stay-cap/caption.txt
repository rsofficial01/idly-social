A US federal judge in Boston has struck down a Department of Homeland Security (DHS) rule that would have capped foreign students' stay in the country at four years. Judge F. Dennis Saylor set the rule aside on Monday (16 November, US time), calling it "arbitrary, capricious, an abuse of discretion", AFP and ANI report.

The rule, published on 17 July, would have ended "duration of status", under which F-1 students stay for as long as their course runs. Students needing more than four years, as many PhD scholars do, would have had to apply to DHS for an extension, and the grace period after a course ends would have fallen from 60 days to 30. Foreign journalists' stays would have been cut to 240 days. A court order on 14 September had stopped the rule from taking effect.

India sends more students to the US than any other country: 3,63,019 in 2024-25, according to the Open Doors report. The US government had appealed the September order to a higher court, ANI reports, and Monday's ruling can also be appealed.
