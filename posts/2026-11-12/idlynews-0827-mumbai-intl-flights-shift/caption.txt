Maharashtra Chief Minister Devendra Fadnavis has written to the Union Civil Aviation Ministry opposing a plan to shift about a third of Mumbai airport's international flights to Navi Mumbai International Airport. In the letter, sent on Wednesday 11 November, he asked that no international flight operating from Mumbai be discontinued or diverted, and said both airports should run independently at their own capacity.

The proposal comes from Mumbai International Airport Ltd (MIAL), the Adani Airport Holdings company that runs the airport. As reported, it would move flights to 14 international destinations, including Doha, Jeddah, Muscat and Riyadh, from the summer 2027 schedule, which starts at the end of March 2027. The operator says the space is needed to rebuild the ageing Terminal 1, which handles 15 million passengers a year. Fadnavis's letter says the current plan would replace it with a new terminal by December 2030.

Fadnavis has proposed a phased rebuild instead: finish the new Terminal 1B first, move Terminal 1A's operations into it, and only then demolish and rebuild 1A, so that flights do not have to leave Mumbai.

IndiGo, Air India and Akasa Air have agreed to move some services to Navi Mumbai, while foreign airlines continue to object. The operator says about 91% of Mumbai's traffic is unaffected by the rebuild.
