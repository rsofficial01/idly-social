Telangana plans to move Classes 11 and 12 out of its separate Intermediate system and into state-run schools from the next academic year, 2027-28. Chief Minister A. Revanth Reddy directed officials to prepare for the change at an education review in Hyderabad on Wednesday 11 November. School would run in three stages, on the lines of CBSE: nursery to Class 4, Classes 5 to 8, and Classes 9 to 12.

The Chief Minister said about 40% of students drop out after Class 10, many of them girls and children from Dalit, tribal and minority communities, and that schools teaching up to Class 12, such as Kendriya Vidyalayas and Navodaya Vidyalayas, see fewer dropouts. He said 18 states already follow the system, and that junior college lecturers and non-teaching staff would keep their service and retirement benefits. No government order on the change has been reported yet.

Classes 11 and 12 now come under the Board of Intermediate Education, which runs under a 1971 law. In May 2026 the Chief Minister deferred the same merger and kept 2026-27 Intermediate admissions as usual. Government junior college lecturers' groups opposed the merger earlier this year and warned of statewide protests.
