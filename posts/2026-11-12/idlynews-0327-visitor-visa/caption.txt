India's Tourist Visa is being renamed the Visitor Visa, and visa application forms are losing 65–70% of their fields, the Ministry of Home Affairs said on 11 November. The new visas are not in use yet: the ministry's statement gives no start date, and officials told The Indian Express that visas under the new framework will be issued by year-end.

The Visitor Visa comes in two kinds. V-1 is valid for 30 days with multiple entries and, besides tourism, covers business meetings, medical consultations, wellness retreats, short treatment and short courses in Indian languages, music, art and craft. V-2 is valid for one, five or 10 years, and mountaineers can get a six-month multiple-entry visa.

Medical visas (M-1, and M-3 for AYUSH treatment) will ordinarily be issued for one year with multiple entries, and attendants travel on M-2 and M-4 visas. Students enrolled through the Study in India portal can get an e-S1 visa for up to five years or the length of their course, whichever is shorter. Business visas split into B-1 for long-term, licence-based work and B-2 for short-term contracts, which includes a Production Investment Business Visa on e-Visa for foreign engineers and technicians hired by Indian companies.

The ministry cut the main visa categories from 13 to nine and sub-categories from 49 to 23 on 27 August, after an October 2025 round took them from 22 to 13. e-Visas are already available to nationals of 172 countries.
