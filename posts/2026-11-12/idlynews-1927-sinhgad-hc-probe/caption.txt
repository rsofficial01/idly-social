The Bombay High Court has ordered the CBI, the Enforcement Directorate and Income Tax authorities to investigate Pune's Sinhgad Technical Education Society (STES), its 44 institutions and its trustees. Teaching and non-teaching staff of those institutions filed about 220 petitions over salaries and terminal dues they say have gone unpaid for almost eight years. The dues are stated in court to exceed ₹400 crore.

In its order of Tuesday 10 November, a special bench of Justices G.S. Kulkarni and Advait M. Sethna kept the bank accounts of the society and its trustees frozen, told the trustees to deposit their passports and not to leave Pune, and issued contempt show-cause notices after STES did not pay the ₹60.65 crore it had undertaken on 6 November to pay staff before Diwali. The court said there was a strong prima facie case that money was being siphoned away from staff while admissions stayed full. That is not a final finding: the allegations are unproven and the trustees can answer them. A response from STES was not reported.

The state Education Department is to take over the society's schools or place their students elsewhere, and administrators are to take charge of its technical colleges. Investigators' first reports are due at the next hearing, on Thursday 19 November.
