India's retail inflation rose to 6.09% in October from 5.54% in September, above the 6% upper limit of the Reserve Bank of India's tolerance band, according to data the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) released on Thursday 12 November.

Food drove the rise. Food inflation climbed to 8.94% from 7.74%. Onion prices were 116.22% higher than a year earlier (108.13% in September), garlic 60.74% and ginger 48.32%. Prices rose faster in villages: rural inflation was 6.63% and urban 5.41%.

The RBI's inflation target is 4%, with a band of 2% to 6%. Its Monetary Policy Committee raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% in October and changed its stance to "calibrated tightening". The committee meets next from 2 to 4 December. Since October 2019 banks have had to link new floating-rate retail loans, such as home loans, to an external benchmark, and the repo rate is one of the options, so repo-linked loan rates move with it.
