Smartphone sales in shops fell 18–20% this Diwali compared with last year, over the four days from Dhanteras (6 November) to 9 November, says the All India Mobile Retailers Association (AIMRA). Spending stayed roughly flat, because the average phone sold cost more.

In the week before Dhanteras, shop sales were down nearly 40% as buyers waited for festive offers. Most buyers spent ₹30,000–60,000 after offers. Realme's sales in shops fell more than 50%, AIMRA says.

Cheaper phones are hit hardest. Shipments of phones under ₹20,000 fell 47% in July–September, Counterpoint says, and many shoppers looking for a phone under ₹15,000 left without buying or chose a pre-owned one.

The cause is memory. AI data centres are buying up memory chips and pushing their cost up. Phone makers are preparing price hikes of up to 15%, and AIMRA expects shop sales to fall by as much as 50% over the next three months.

Big retail chains that stocked up before the price rises offered 12–15% off this season, and about 60% of festive phone purchases were financed.
