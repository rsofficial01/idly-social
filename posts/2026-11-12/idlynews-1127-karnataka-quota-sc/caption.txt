Karnataka has filed a Special Leave Petition in the Supreme Court against the Karnataka High Court's 12 October verdict that struck down its 2022 law raising reservation for Scheduled Castes from 15% to 17% and for Scheduled Tribes from 3% to 7%. With OBC reservation unchanged at 32%, the law had taken the state's total reservation from 50% to 56%. The Chief Minister's Office announced the appeal on Wednesday 11 November.

A division bench of Chief Justice Vibhu Bakhru and Justice K.S. Hemalekha held that the increase breached the 50% ceiling on reservation, and that a state cannot cross it simply because communities are under-represented. It also held that the state had to consult the National Commissions for Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes before making the law; letters sent about three years after it was enacted did not count.

The case touches state jobs. In February 2026 Karnataka approved recruitment to 56,432 posts and kept an additional 6% of SC/ST posts in a backlog category pending the court's decision. The state also wants the 56% quota placed in the Ninth Schedule of the Constitution: the Assembly asked the Centre for this on 18 December 2025, and the Centre told Parliament on 25 July 2026 that it would defer the request while the case was before the High Court. The precedent is Tamil Nadu, whose 69% reservation law was placed in the Ninth Schedule by the Constitution's 76th Amendment in 1994. No Supreme Court hearing date has been reported.
