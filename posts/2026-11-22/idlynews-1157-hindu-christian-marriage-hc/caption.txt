The Madras High Court has held that a marriage between a Hindu and a Christian cannot be solemnised or registered under the Hindu Marriage Act, 1955, because the Act covers only marriages between two Hindus. A Division Bench of Justices CV Karthikeyan and S Raveekumar declared void a February 2009 registration of a Christian man's marriage to a Hindu woman, set aside a 2019 family court order, and directed that the judgment be circulated to marriage registrars across Tamil Nadu.

The bench said registration under Section 8 of the Act cannot validate a marriage that Section 5 does not permit, and that Tamil Nadu's self-respect marriages under Section 7A also need both partners to be Hindu. It pointed to two other routes: the Special Marriage Act, 1954, or, where a partner is Christian, the Indian Christian Marriage Act, 1872. The court accepted the man's baptism records and said a person's religion cannot be decided from a name alone.

Under the Special Marriage Act, a couple gives notice to the marriage officer of the district where one partner has lived for at least 30 days. Objections can be raised within 30 days of the notice being published, and the marriage is solemnised before the officer and three witnesses.
