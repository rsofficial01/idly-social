Voters dropped from the electoral roll who apply again on Form 6 will not be prosecuted for that alone, the Election Commission (EC) has told the Supreme Court.

In an affidavit filed on 14 November, the EC said its 3 November letter to every state's Chief Electoral Officer makes clear that filing Form 6 to get back on the roll during or after the Special Intensive Revision (SIR) is not, by itself, a false declaration, even for someone enrolled before. The one condition: the person must not be enrolled in another constituency. A false declaration about the voter roll is punishable under Section 31 of the Representation of the People Act, 1950 with up to a year in jail, a fine, or both.

Form 6 is meant for first-time voters, and the EC says some people left out of SIR draft rolls feared that using it would count against them. It has told states to publicise the clarification in local languages, and says voters left off draft rolls as absent, shifted, dead or duplicate will get another chance to be included in the final rolls.

Separately, in 13 states and three Union Territories where the SIR is over, draft voter lists under the annual Special Summary Revision came out on 12 November. The EC's notice says people deleted during the SIR can file Form 6 there until 11 December; final lists are due on 4 January 2027.
