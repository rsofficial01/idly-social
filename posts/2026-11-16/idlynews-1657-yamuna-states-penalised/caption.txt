Any state, Union Territory or district where the Yamuna's water gets dirtier as it flows through "shall be held accountable and penalised", the Supreme Court has said. A bench of Justices Manoj Misra and Manmohan said that despite huge sums spent on cleaning the river, "pollution in the Yamuna has only increased".

The Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB) is to test the water where the river and its major drains enter and leave every state and district, and suggest a formula for fines that states can recover from polluters and negligent officials. Every sewage treatment plant, common effluent treatment plant and government biogas plant must fit tamper-proof smart meters and flow sensors within a month and put live data online; a data gap of more than 24 hours will be presumed to mean the plant was not running. Chief Secretaries of the Yamuna basin states must name a nodal officer within two weeks.

The court noted that 520 drains discharge wastewater into the river, and that none of Delhi's 37 existing sewage plants met all prescribed norms, according to a Central Pollution Control Board report. The order is dated 4 November; the next hearing is on 13 January 2027.
