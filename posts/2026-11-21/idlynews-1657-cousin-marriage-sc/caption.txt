The Supreme Court has asked the Centre to consider whether India needs a law, or wider public awareness, on the health risks of marriages between blood relatives. A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana issued a limited notice on Friday on a petition by a 20-year-old law student whose parents are first cousins. Her counsel told the court she has 40% visual impairment.

The court said making laws on marriage customs is for Parliament, and that it was not directing the government to legislate. It said the Centre could also look at public outreach and school and college curricula.

The petition asks for genetic counselling before marriage in areas where such marriages are common, voluntary screening and a nationwide awareness campaign, and says existing marriages should not be criminalised.

In Tamil Nadu, 28% of ever-married women aged 15-49 are married to a blood relative, the highest share of any state, followed by Karnataka at 27%, according to the National Family Health Survey (NFHS-5, 2019-21). The national figure is about 11%.
