Congress MP Shashi Tharoor says 115 of the 315 foreign speakers cleared by the Ministry of External Affairs for the Bharat Summit in Hyderabad were refused entry to India or had their visas cancelled, "apparently on the instructions of the Ministry of Home Affairs". In a post on X on Friday, he said it was "completely unclear under what provision of law" the government had acted.

The two-day summit, hosted by Telangana's Congress-led government at HITEX, ends on Saturday, 14 November. Organisers say about 200 international delegates attended. Telangana BJP president N. Ramchander Rao has called it an "anti-Bharat" summit.

Asked about the reports at his weekly briefing on Friday, MEA spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said: "Visa matters are sovereign functions of any country or any government." The MEA's letter clearing the foreign names on 3 November said clearance from the Ministry of Home Affairs and other relevant authorities was also required, The Wire reports. No Home Ministry statement has been reported.

Norwegian journalist Helle Lyng Svendsen of Dagsavisen, who was due to speak on press freedom, says the Indian embassy in Oslo told her about 10 minutes before she left for the airport that her visa was cancelled. In May, she asked PM Narendra Modi in Oslo whether he would take questions from the press. The Association of Norwegian Editors and the Norwegian Press Association have asked the embassy whether the two are linked.
