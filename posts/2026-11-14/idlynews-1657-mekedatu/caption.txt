Karnataka Chief Minister D.K. Shivakumar asked the Centre on Friday to help clear the Mekedatu balancing reservoir on the Cauvery, at the inauguration of CoSMOS (Centre of Space and Modern Optical Sciences) in Mysuru, with Prime Minister Narendra Modi present. He said the Supreme Court had given a clear verdict that Mekedatu "has to be built in Karnataka" and that "no one can stop it", and added that the drinking-water reservoir would help Tamil Nadu too.

On Saturday, Shivakumar said Karnataka would still give Tamil Nadu its share of water under the Supreme Court's ruling. "It is my state, my right," he said. Karnataka puts the project at ₹16,745 crore (PTI, IANS).

Tamil Nadu's government opposes the dam. In a letter to the Prime Minister on 7 November, Chief Minister Vijay asked that no clearance be given without the consent of the other Cauvery basin states, saying the planned 67.16 TMC reservoir just upstream of the border could cut monthly flows to Tamil Nadu. The state Assembly passed a unanimous resolution against the dam on 19 June. On Saturday, Tamil Nadu Congress president Manickam Tagore called Shivakumar's remarks "deeply condemnable" and said approval without Tamil Nadu's consent would be illegal, and DMK president M.K. Stalin wrote to Vijay asking for an all-party meeting and a joint delegation to the Prime Minister. Shivakumar and Tagore are both Congress leaders.

On 13 November 2025, the Supreme Court rejected Tamil Nadu's applications against the project as premature: the Central Water Commission (CWC) had only allowed a detailed project report, and if the CWC approves it, Tamil Nadu is free to take steps permitted in law. Karnataka sent its report to the CWC on 13 August 2026. Under the court's 2018 Cauvery verdict, Karnataka must release 177.25 TMC a year to Tamil Nadu at Biligundlu.
