India's Haj quota for 2027 is 1,75,025 pilgrims, unchanged since Haj 2023. India and Saudi Arabia signed the bilateral Haj agreement for 2027 in Jeddah on Monday, 9 November, after talks between Minority Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju and Saudi Hajj and Umrah Minister Tawfiq bin Fawzan Al Rabiah, the Indian Consulate in Jeddah said.

Under Haj Policy 2027, announced in June, the seats are split 70:30: 1,22,518 for pilgrims who go through the Haj Committee of India and 52,507 for private tour operators.

The policy keeps the 20-day Short Haj package, first offered for Haj 2026, and adds Kolkata as a new embarkation point for it. It also raises the number of state Haj inspectors to one for every 135 pilgrims, from one for every 150.

India's quota was 2,00,000 in 2019. In June, the Centre said it would keep working to get the quota raised. Saudi Arabia sets each country's quota every year, as space at the holy sites is limited.
