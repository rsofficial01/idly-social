The Supreme Court has held that when a Hindu man dies without a will and his property is divided among his Class I heirs under Section 8 of the Hindu Succession Act, 1956, the share his son receives becomes the son's own property, not joint family property. The grandson has no right to it by birth and cannot sue for partition while the father is alive.

Justices Augustine George Masih and Sunita Agarwal dismissed a grandson's appeal on 6 November 2026 (Civil Appeal 7020 of 2012). His father got a 1,811 sq ft plot under a 1994 compromise decree dividing the grandfather's estate and sold it in 1997. The grandson's partition suit, filed through his mother that year in a Chennai civil court, won at trial, but an appeal court set that decree aside and the Madras High Court upheld that.

The court noted that a son's son is not a Class I heir under the Act, while the son of a predeceased son is.
