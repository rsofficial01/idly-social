The US State Department has delivered Gurpatwant Singh Pannun's civil lawsuit against the Government of India to the Indian Embassy in Washington. In a letter filed in a New York federal court on 28 October, it said the papers went under a diplomatic note delivered on 9 October, and that it takes no position on the case.

Pannun, whom India has designated a terrorist, is a lawyer for Sikhs for Justice, which India has banned. He filed the suit in September 2024 and alleges that Indian officials plotted to kill him in New York in 2023. The suit also names National Security Adviser Ajit Doval and former R&AW chief Samant Goel. On 9 November, a US federal judge in New York allowed him to serve the two through courts in Delhi, with his papers for that route due by 30 November.

India rejected the suits on 10 November. "We reject the fabricated and malicious attacks on our high officials. As also, we reject the frivolous lawsuits," Ministry of External Affairs spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal said, adding that requests to serve judicial documents in India can only be routed through the Ministry of Law and Justice. In a separate US criminal case, Indian national Nikhil Gupta pleaded guilty in February to murder-for-hire charges over the alleged plot.
