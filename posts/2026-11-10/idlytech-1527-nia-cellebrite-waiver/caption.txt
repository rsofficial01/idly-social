Home Minister Amit Shah approved a one-time exemption from India's Make in India procurement order so the National Investigation Agency (NIA) could buy a Cellebrite phone-unlocking tool, documents published by the Internet Freedom Foundation (IFF) show.

A Ministry of Home Affairs letter dated 20 January 2026 records the approval. IFF says it has not seen the Minister's 17 January note itself and has filed an RTI request for it.

The NIA's bid, as published by IFF, is for one Cellebrite Premium tool on a three-year licence, estimated at ₹4,98,15,000. The contract went to Nextechno Gen Pvt Ltd for ₹4,97,99,515, IFF and MediaNama report. A certificate in the bid names Israel's Cellebrite DI Ltd as the only maker and states: "No other make or model is acceptable."

IFF found Cellebrite products named in 15 listings in 11 government tenders this year, among them a Serious Fraud Investigation Office (SFIO) bid that lists Israel as the country of origin. Neither IFF nor MediaNama carries a response from the MHA, the NIA or Cellebrite.
