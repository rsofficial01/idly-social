The Madurai Bench of the Madras High Court on Tuesday directed Tamil Nadu's Directorate of Vigilance and Anti-Corruption (DVAC) to register an FIR over the alleged fraudulent purchase of books for the state's public libraries, including books alleged to be AI-generated. The interim directions came in a contempt case over the court's June 2024 order on transparent book buying.

The court also directed that a publisher's bank accounts be frozen, that the officials responsible be identified, and that librarians make suspect books unavailable to readers. It said "crores of rupees" had gone out of the public exchequer, and that AI-generated and plagiarised works fall within "doubtful taste" under the Tamil Nadu Public Libraries Rules, 1950. The court cited a book at Madurai's Kalaignar Centenary Library falsely credited to economist Jean Drèze, who has called it fake, and works falsely attributed to poet Subramania Bharati. The matter came to light after writer Perumal Murugan raised it over books bought for Coimbatore's Periyar Arivulagam library. The case returns to court on 11 November.
