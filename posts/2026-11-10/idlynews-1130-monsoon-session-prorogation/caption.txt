Parliament's Monsoon Session has not been formally closed 89 days after both Houses were adjourned sine die on 13 August. PTI and Congress's Jairam Ramesh say no session has stayed unprorogued this long.

Adjournment sine die ends sittings without fixing a date to meet again, but the session stays open. Prorogation, ordered by the President under Article 85 of the Constitution, formally ends it, and a new session then needs a fresh summons. Article 85 allows no more than six months between the last sitting of one session and the first sitting of the next.

Congress alleges the session is being kept open so the government can find a two-thirds Lok Sabha majority for the delimitation bills. "Today is 88 days since Parliament was adjourned sine die and has still NOT been prorogued," Ramesh posted on X on Monday.

Parliamentary Affairs Minister Kiren Rijiju wrote to Congress president Mallikarjun Kharge on 19 October that "there is no constitutional requirement prescribing a specific period within which a House must be prorogued following its adjournment sine die". He said the government had consulted the DMK, SP, YSRCP, NCP, Shiv Sena (UBT), AIMIM and BJD on delimitation.

On 17 April, the Constitution (131st Amendment) Bill, 2026, which would redraw constituencies and amend the 2023 women's reservation law, got 298 votes to 230 in the Lok Sabha. It needed 352, two-thirds of the 528 members present.
