Oxford University's Pitt Rivers Museum handed back the remains of 39 Naga ancestors, mostly skulls, at a ceremony at the museum on Monday, 9 November. The remains are on their way to Nagaland, where Naga tribal bodies plan to bury them together at the Naga Solidarity Park in Kohima. Preparatory work on the burial site has begun.

Most of the remains reached Oxford between about 1910 and 1940, brought mainly by J.H. Hutton and J.P. Mills, colonial administrators and anthropologists. The museum says such remains were "frequently confiscated during punitive military expeditions, rather than freely given". It took all human remains off display in 2020.

The return follows a Naga-led process that began in 2020, facilitated by the Forum for Naga Reconciliation with the Naga Tribe Hohos. Oxford declined one further remain that the museum says lacks a clear provenance as Naga. The museum also holds 178 objects that contain, or may contain, human hair; these are not part of this return. It says it holds about 6,500 Naga objects.

Museum director Laura Van Broekhoven called the handover a "historic moment that will be part of stories told to next generations", AFP reported. Thejao Vihienuo, president of the Angami Public Organisation, who represented the Naga Tribe Hoho, said: "We shall put them to respectful rest." PTI reported that he called for all Naga ancestral remains held in museums to be returned.
