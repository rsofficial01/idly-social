In an order dated 6 November, the Supreme Court told the Delhi, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana and Rajasthan governments, plus the MCD and Delhi Police (Traffic Department), to file detailed responses on vehicle pollution in Delhi-NCR before its next hearing on 23 November.

The bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana wants to know how vehicles entering from neighbouring states are regulated, how PUC rules are enforced, what is being done about over-age and end-of-life vehicles, and how many vehicles were challaned or denied entry. Each authority must also give a "time-bound roadmap" for stronger enforcement.

GRAP compliance data that the amici curiae placed before the court for 21 October to 3 November showed a substantial decline in stubble-burning incidents across Delhi, UP, Haryana and Rajasthan. The same data, the order says, also shows "the sharp increase in issues attributable to vehicular emissions". "There can be little doubt that the issue warrants urgent and immediate intervention," the court said.

Additional Solicitor General Aishwarya Bhati, for the Union government and the Commission for Air Quality Management, highlighted steps already taken, including restrictions on vehicle emissions and measures to cut stubble burning. The states were given two weeks to put on record what they have done, their responses to the suggestions of the CAQM and the amici, and what they plan next. The amici curiae pointed out that compliance with the court's earlier orders of 21 January and 23 February 2026 is yet to be properly reported.

The court will take up the other parts of the case on 25 November (solid waste, crop residue and firecrackers), 26 November (construction, power plants and other industries) and 30 November (green cover).
