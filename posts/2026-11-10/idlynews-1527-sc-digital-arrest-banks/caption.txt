RBI's rules must make banks and other financial institutions repay digital arrest victims at least what they lost when it is established the fraud happened because the institution failed in due diligence, the Supreme Court has said. Its written order, reported on 10 November, now sets out who must act next.

A bench of Chief Justice Surya Kant and Justices Joymalya Bagchi and V Mohana said so at the 29 October hearing: the Reserve Bank of India's proposed rules must require "suitable compensation not less than the amount lost by the customer" where the institution failed "to exercise due diligence in preventing or reporting" the fraud. The court said it would first examine RBI's KYC amendment directions, which are proposed to be notified by 30 November and put into operation by 31 December.

The order directs WhatsApp and other intermediaries to tell the court what they propose to do against digital arrest scams, and to send the same proposals to the Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C). The RBI must list all authorised financial institutions, and police in every state and union territory must report on digital arrest FIRs and investigations. The court has been told that 9,400 WhatsApp accounts allegedly linked to such scams were banned. The case returns on 23 November.

In February 2026, the same court described the more than ₹54,000 crore lost to digital frauds as "robbery or dacoity".

To report a financial cyber fraud, the Home Ministry's I4C lists the national helpline 1930, with the complaint completed on cybercrime.gov.in.
