The RBI has given Paytm a first nod to run digital wallets again. On 13 November it granted Paytm Payments Services, a wholly owned subsidiary of One 97 Communications, in-principle authorisation to issue prepaid payment instruments, including wallets, under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007.

This is not a wallet you can use yet. Paytm must complete a system audit, including a cybersecurity audit, and file the report with the RBI within six months, or the nod lapses automatically. It can issue wallets only after the RBI grants a final Certificate of Authorisation. Paytm applied for the licence in July 2026.

The old Paytm wallet ran through Paytm Payments Bank. RBI curbs in 2024 stopped top-ups, though users could still withdraw their balances, and the RBI cancelled the bank's licence on 24 April 2026. One 97 Communications shares touched a 52-week high of ₹1,960 on Friday, Times of India and India Today report.

If you still had money in an old Paytm wallet: the Delhi High Court ordered Paytm Payments Bank wound up in July, and an official liquidator, former SBI chief general manager Girikumar M. Nair, has exercised the bank's board powers since 8 July. The RBI has said the bank has enough liquidity to repay its entire deposit liability. Paytm's UPI and QR payments run through partner banks and were not affected by the bank's closure.
