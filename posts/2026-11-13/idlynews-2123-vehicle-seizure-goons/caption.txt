A finance company can take back a vehicle over a loan default only through a civil court or an arbitrator, not by force, the Allahabad High Court has held. Justices J.J. Munir and Indrajeet Shukla ruled on 26 October on a petition against HDB Financial Services; Bar & Bench, LawChakra and NewsX reported the judgment on Friday 13 November.

The owner had financed his vehicle through HDB over 45 monthly instalments and says he had paid 31 when men acting for the lender stopped him on 9 August 2026 and took it; his petition alleges they did it at gunpoint. HDB told the court his loan account was linked to his brother's defaulted loan and that his own cheques had bounced. The court noted that HDB had taken the brother's case to an arbitrator but had started no such case against him.

Taking a vehicle back under a loan contract "cannot be done by force employing goons, styled as agents", the court said, adding that allowing it would arm a private party with the coercive authority of the State. It ordered the vehicle returned without any precondition, the loan restructured at the usual interest with no penal interest, and ₹10,000 in costs to the owner. If he defaults again, the lender can act only through a civil court or the arbitrator.

The bench relied on the Supreme Court's 16 September ruling in Hari Dutta Sharma v State of UP. There, a lender took a truck at about 1 am without the seven days' notice its loan agreement required. The Supreme Court ordered it to pay the owner ₹10 lakh for mental agony and lost livelihood and to refund the ₹4.5 lakh sale price with 6% interest, and asked the RBI to make banks and NBFCs follow its recovery rules. A High Court ruling binds the courts in its own state; a Supreme Court ruling binds every court in India.
