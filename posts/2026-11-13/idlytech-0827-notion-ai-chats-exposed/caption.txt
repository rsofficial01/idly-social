Notion says a hack at Fireworks AI, a company that ran some of the AI models behind Notion AI, exposed Notion AI chat transcripts from fewer than 1% of Notion workspaces. The transcripts hold what people typed into Notion AI chats and the replies, and came from data used for Insights, the admin view of how a team uses AI. Notion says most of them cannot be tied to a specific user or workspace.

Notion says it cut its systems off from Fireworks and deleted the data held there on 3 November, and has switched Insights off while it investigates. It says Fireworks kept the data despite a contractual zero-data-retention commitment. Fireworks says the attackers came in through a third-party cloud provider; TechCrunch and BleepingComputer report it is Fireworks' second breach in about two months.

Notion's advice, in its advisory of 9 November: in affected workspaces, anyone who pasted passwords, API keys or other credentials into Notion AI should change them right away. Workspace owners can see the affected data on a computer (not the mobile app) from the help icon in the sidebar, then Security incident review. If neither you nor your workspace owner has heard from Notion, it says no action is needed for now.
