Victims of a road accident caused by an "Act of God" are still entitled to no-fault compensation, the Punjab and Haryana High Court has ruled, LiveLaw and NewsX reported on Thursday 12 November.

On 9 March 1997, a mass of ice fell on a car near Lakkar Mandi, Dalhousie, in Himachal Pradesh, and pushed it into a gorge. The owner, who was driving, died and the four other people in the car were grievously hurt. In March 2004 the Motor Accident Claims Tribunal in Amritsar dismissed all five claims, calling it an Act of God with no negligence by any driver. The appeals had been pending in the High Court since then.

Justice Parmod Goyal agreed that negligence wasn't proved, but held that the injuries arose from the use of an insured vehicle, which is enough for no-fault compensation under Section 164 of the Motor Vehicles Act: "No fault liability shall include even 'Act of God'." Each of the four injured gets ₹2.5 lakh, with 6% interest from the date of their claim. The court applied Section 164, in force since 1 April 2022, to the older accident, relying on Supreme Court rulings. Section 164 fixes ₹5 lakh where a victim dies.

The claim over the owner-driver's death failed. The court accepted that he was not a "third party", and his 1997 policy covered only a paid driver, with no premium paid to cover the owner.
