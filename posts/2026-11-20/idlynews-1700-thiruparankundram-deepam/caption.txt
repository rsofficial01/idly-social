The Supreme Court on Friday allowed a lamp to be lit on 24 November, Karthigai Deepam day, at the disputed stone pillar on Thiruparankundram hill in Madurai, for this year only. A bench of Justices Aravind Kumar and Vipul M Pancholi said only a small temple team may go up, chosen by the Madurai Collector with the temple's executive officer, the Archaeological Survey of India and the police commissioner. The general public is barred from the site, as are gatherings, processions, demonstrations and slogans. Worship at customary sites and lawful access to the dargah and mosque on the hill must continue undisturbed.

The court said the permission is not recognition of a custom or a right of access, and cannot be relied on in later years. It stayed the Madras High Court's findings on the nature of the pillar and the land it stands on. The temple side calls the pillar a Deepathoon, a lamp pillar; the State side argues it was a survey stone; the dargah committee relies on a 1923 decree for its rights on the hilltop. The appeals are listed for the second week of January 2027.

The temple administration has said the lamp's traditional site is near the Uchi Pillaiyar temple on the hill. In December 2025 a single judge of the High Court ordered it lit at the pillar too; police did not allow it that year, and the then DMK government appealed. A division bench upheld the order on 6 January 2026, and the TVK government moved the Supreme Court on 11 June.
