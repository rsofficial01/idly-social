Foreign portfolio investors put a net ₹53,739 crore into Indian shares from 2 to 9 November, six trading sessions, according to depository (NSDL) data reported by PTI. That follows net selling of ₹35,861 crore in September and ₹34,843 crore in October.

They are still net sellers for the year: cumulative foreign outflows from Indian equities in 2026 stand at ₹2.41 lakh crore, the same data show. Over 2–9 November they were major buyers of index heavyweights, especially in financial services, IT, autos and telecom.

V K Vijayakumar, chief investment strategist at Geojit Investments, links the turn to a sell-off in AI-linked stocks in the US, South Korea, Taiwan and Japan, which made India, seen as a non-AI market, more attractive. He also points to better-than-expected July–September company results, and market reports cite easing crude oil prices.

On Wednesday, 11 November, the first session after Tuesday's market holiday, the Sensex rose more than 1,000 points in early trade, with PTI citing foreign fund inflows, July–September results and softer crude.
